Check for UnderstandingWB pp. 47-52

Reading Comprehension Answers

Answers to the Check for Understanding questions, in workbook order. The Student Edition page reference printed on each workbook page is shown on the card.

1
SE pp. 346-350WB p. 47

Why were wolves removed from Yellowstone long ago?

Correct answer

  • a.They wanted to protect the wolves from other predators.
  • b.Wolves were overpopulated in Yellowstone.
  • ✓c.The government thought they were harmful.
  • d.They started attacking human tourists and farmers.

Support (p. 346): the U.S. government thought wolves did more harm than good, so they were banned from the park.

2
SE pp. 346-350WB p. 47

What was the government’s solution for controlling the wolf population in the early 1900s?

Model answer

It started a program to control predators, and hunters killed the wolves. By 1926 the last Yellowstone wolf pack had been killed, and for decades only a few solitary wolves were sighted.

Look for: a predator-control program; hunters killing the wolves; last pack gone by 1926 (pp. 346-347).

3
SE pp. 346-350WB p. 47

Match each cause with its correct effect.

Correct matches

  • Elk population increases.→Beavers have less materials to build dams.
  • Wolves are reintroduced.→Elk population decreases.
  • More willow trees grow.→Beavers return to Yellowstone.
  • Wolves attract ecotourists.→More tourists visit Yellowstone.
4
SE pp. 348-349WB p. 48

Which image is the best example of biodiversity?

Correct answer

  • a.a. City square crowded with pigeons
  • b.b. Empty desert with sand dunes
  • ✓c.c. Rainforest full of many different plants and animals
  • d.d. Forest meadow with a few wolves

Biodiversity is the number of species in an ecosystem, and a healthy ecosystem has many different species (p. 348). The rainforest shows the greatest variety of species.

5
SE pp. 348-349WB p. 48

Circle the word that best completes each sentence: When wolves were eliminated, the number of elk [multiplied / halved]. The elk were eating too many trees, which made it [easy / hard] for beavers to survive. Then, when wolves were [reintroduced / eliminated], biodiversity improved.

Circle

multiplied / hard / reintroduced

6
SE pp. 349-351WB p. 49

Which of the following is NOT a benefit of having wolves at Yellowstone?

Correct answer

  • a.Improvement of biodiversity
  • b.Positive impact on Yellowstone’s economy
  • c.Demonstration of wolves and humans coexisting
  • ✓d.Decreasing the population of predators that threaten wildlife

The three benefits the author gives are biodiversity, the economy (ecotourism), and coexistence. Decreasing predators was the old reason for removing wolves, not a benefit of bringing them back.

7
SE pp. 349-351WB p. 49

What was the scientist Daniel Licht’s opinion on wolves?

Correct answer

  • a.He supported bringing back the wolves, but was concerned.
  • ✓b.He believed strongly that wolves should be returned to the park.
  • c.He was very worried about the negative impact of wolves.
  • d.He was bothered by the ecotourists disturbing scientific research.

Licht said ecosystems are in dire need of wolves and that the benefits would far outweigh the negatives (p. 350). Note: the scientist who supported wolves but was concerned is Doug Smith in the other text, not Licht.

8
SE pp. 349-351WB p. 49
Open / persuasive response

How would you persuade people who believe wolves are harmful to humans?

Answers may vary

Look for: Uses objective facts from the text rather than emotion, for example: no wolf has attacked a human at Yellowstone; only a tiny percent of livestock die from wolf attacks; the “big, bad wolf” is a myth (p. 351).

9
SE pp. 355-357WB p. 50

Which two (2) groups of people were against having wolves in Yellowstone? Put a check in the correct boxes.

Check these two

  • Scientists
  • ✓Ranchers
  • ✓Farmers
  • Campers
  • Tourists
  • Hotel owners

Farmers and ranchers speak most loudly against wolves because wolves threaten their livestock (p. 355).

10
SE pp. 355-357WB p. 50

According to the text, why do wolves create a “landscape of fear” for cattle?

Correct answer

  • a.Wolves chase cattle away from their food sources.
  • b.Wolves live among the cattle and scare them at night.
  • ✓c.Cattle spend more time watching for wolves and always stay alert.
  • d.Wolves dig holes in grazing areas, making it harder for cattle to move.

Cattle stay on the alert while grazing, spending less time eating and more time looking around for danger (p. 356).

11
SE pp. 355-357WB p. 50

Why do some ranchers oppose the reintroduction of wolves?

Correct answer

  • a.Wolves increase competition for food.
  • b.Wolf habitat takes up land that could be used for farming.
  • ✓c.Wolves attack livestock and cause financial losses.
  • d.Wolves make the land unsafe for farming.

Wolves kill livestock, and frightened cattle fail to gain weight, so ranchers make less money (pp. 355-357).

12
SE pp. 356-359WB p. 51

How does the author describe the effect of wolves on grizzly bears?

Correct answer

  • a.Wolves decrease the grizzly population by competing for food.
  • ✓b.Wolves help increase the grizzly population by leaving prey behind for them to eat.
  • c.Wolves and grizzlies work together to hunt prey.
  • d.Wolves force grizzlies to leave Yellowstone.

Grizzlies are scavengers that eat the prey wolves leave behind, so more wolves means more food and a larger grizzly population (p. 358).

13
SE pp. 356-359WB p. 51

What is one argument against wolves improving Yellowstone’s ecosystem?

Correct answer

  • a.Wolves leave too much for the grizzly bears to scavenge.
  • ✓b.Wolves may not affect the number of trees.
  • c.Wolves help increase biodiveristy during droughts.
  • d.Wolves wander to locations remote from Yellowstone.

Some scientists say the trees recovered because a drought ended, not because of the wolves, so the wolves may not have affected the trees after all (p. 359).

14
SE pp. 356-359WB p. 51

What is the author’s summary of her argument against wolves in Yellowstone?

Correct answer

  • a.Wolves help balance the ecosystem and should stay.
  • b.Wolves should only be protected outside of the park.
  • c.Wolves have no effect on Yellowstone’s environment.
  • ✓d.Wolves do more harm than good and create new problems in the park.

The author concludes wolves are not the answer; they bring new problems, and Yellowstone would be better off without them (p. 359).

15
SE pp. 351, 355-356WB p. 52

Who do the wolves create problems for according to both texts?

Model answer

Farmers and ranchers, and their livestock (cattle). Both texts agree that wolves threaten the livestock of farmers and ranchers on the lands near Yellowstone.

Look for: names farmers and ranchers (and their cattle/livestock); notes both texts agree on this point.

16
SE both textsWB p. 52

What is a big difference between the two texts?

Correct answer

  • ✓a.They have different conclusions.
  • b.One is fictional and the other is nonfictional.
  • c.One is informational and the other is persuasive.
  • d.They are talking about completely different topics.

Both are persuasive nonfiction on the same topic, but they reach opposite conclusions: one argues to welcome wolves back, the other argues wolves do not belong.

CR-1
SE p. 346
Analyze Text Structure

Underline the sentence that helps you identify the author’s claim about wolves in Yellowstone.

Look for

“Returning wolves to Yellowstone was the right decision for several reasons.” (last sentence of paragraph 1)

CR-2
SE p. 347
Compare and Contrast Texts

Highlight facts about the government’s program on wolves in Yellowstone that agree with paragraph 1 of Wolves Don’t Belong in Yellowstone.

Look for

The early-1900s predator-control program and the killing of the wolves: that the government started a program to control predators, and that by 1926 the last Yellowstone wolf pack had been killed. These agree with the other text’s statement that the government eliminated most wolves in the early 1900s.

CR-3
SE p. 348
Compare and Contrast Texts

Highlight a key idea about the value of wolves in Yellowstone that contrasts with the information in Wolves Don’t Belong in Yellowstone.

Look for

That wolves improve biodiversity (paragraph 5). This contrasts with the other text’s claim that wolves may not improve Yellowstone’s ecosystem.

CR-4
SE p. 349
Analyze Text Structure

Underline a detail that supports the author’s claim that wolves improve biodiversity.

Look for

Any step in the chain in paragraphs 6-7, for example: the wolves returned and ate elk, willows grew back, and beavers returned to the park, so biodiversity improved.

CR-5
SE p. 350
Analyze Text Structure

Underline the author’s opinion about the effect of ecotourists who visit Yellowstone.

Look for

“In other words, wolves help people pay their bills. That’s a good thing!” (paragraph 10)

CR-6
SE p. 351
Compare and Contrast Texts

Highlight details that suggest that the author’s purpose for writing this text is different from the author’s purpose for writing Wolves Don’t Belong in Yellowstone.

Look for

“Wolves belong at Yellowstone” and “the good they do far outweighs the risks. Welcome back, wolves!” The purpose here is to persuade readers that wolves should be in Yellowstone, the opposite of the other author’s purpose.

CR-7
SE p. 354
Analyze Text Structure

Underline the sentence that helps you identify the author’s claim about wolves in Yellowstone.

Look for

“Gray wolves are too dangerous to roam in Yellowstone National Park.” (Also acceptable: “Removing the wolves was the right decision.”)

CR-8
SE p. 355
Compare and Contrast Texts

Highlight a key detail about farmers and ranchers that is the same in both texts.

Look for

That farmers and ranchers oppose wolves because wolves threaten their livestock: “Farmers and ranchers speak most loudly against wolves in Yellowstone.” Both texts make this point.

CR-9
SE p. 356
Vocabulary in Context

Use a context clue beyond the sentence to determine the meaning of alert. Underline the context clue that supports your definition.

Look for

alert = watchful, on guard for danger. Context clue: “They spend less time eating and more time looking around for danger.”

CR-10
SE p. 357
Analyze Text Structure

Underline the statements in the quote by Doug Smith that are opinions, not facts.

Look for

“Life is simpler without wolves.”; “having wolves around is worrisome”; and “I think that’s the best argument to not have them.” (Signal words: simpler, worrisome, I think.)

CR-11
SE p. 358
Analyze Text Structure

Underline claims you identify that are meant to persuade readers that wolves in Yellowstone cause problems.

Look for

“Wolves increase Yellowstone’s grizzly bear population” and “Grizzlies are far more likely to attack people than wolves are.” (Also: “There’s another problem too.”)

CR-12
SE p. 359
Compare and Contrast Texts

Highlight key details of the author’s message that contrast with the author’s message in Welcome Back, Wolves!

Look for

That “wolves may not improve Yellowstone’s ecosystem” and that “Wolves are not the answer to Yellowstone’s problems. Instead, they bring new problems. Yellowstone National Park would be better off without them.”
